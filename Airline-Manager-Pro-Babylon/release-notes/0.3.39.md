# 0.3.39

## Your airport: Mýrdalssandur (BIAP / VTX)
- Your terminal now has its own airport, named after the place it stands: Mýrdalssandur Airport, Iceland, ICAO BIAP, IATA VTX. It is in every airport list (search BIAP, VTX or Mýrdalssandur) with its runway.
- Runway 08/26: 4,000 m by 60 m, Code 4F (Airbus A380), with displaced thresholds (take-off run 4,000 m, landing distance 3,750 m each way), the threshold, designator, aiming point and touchdown zone markings, stopways at both ends.
- Taxiways A and B parallel on both sides, 260 m from the runway, with gently curved ends (A1/A3, B1/B3) and the middle exits A2/B2; apron links P1–P3 and Q1–Q2; taxilanes T (terminal) and K (cargo).
- Holding positions at every link (pattern A) with their red mandatory signs, location signs and runway guard lights that flash in turn; direction signs on the taxiways and the aprons.
- The terminal apron in asphalt with stands 1–8 at the pier (jetway markings at stands 1–4, painted walkways at 5–8) and the A380 stand 9; the concrete cargo apron with stands C1–C4 and the cargo terminal.

## Airport (in the menu)
- The whole airfield is built around your terminal at its site, with the same graphics as its photo renders: one seamless ground (grass, asphalt and concrete worked out from their place, grain, sealed cracks, dirt, rubber in the touchdown zones, tyre and oil marks on the stands), every marking painted on it (worn, chipped, rubbered; thin lines stay visible from far away), the signs and the lights.
- Inside the airport boundary the ground is levelled, blending into the real ground only within its outer 100 m; outside it the real ground and the mountains are untouched. The fence runs along the boundary.
- New views: threshold 08, holding point A1, the touchdown zone, middle exit A2, the A380 stand, the cargo apron, from the air and a low fly-by.

## The map and the 3D view
- Zoomed in on the map (and in the airport view) Mýrdalssandur is drawn from above: the runway, taxiways and links with their curves, the aprons, the buildings, the stands with their numbers, the holding positions and the taxiways' names.
- In the 3D view of a flight to or from BIAP the same airfield is there, with its ground, markings, signs and lights, the terminal as blocks.
