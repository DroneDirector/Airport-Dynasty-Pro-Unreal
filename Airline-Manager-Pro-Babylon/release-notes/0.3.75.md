# 0.3.75

## Furniture and fittings: far less work for the processor
- The seats, chairs, tables, shelves, lockers, plants, carts, lamps and other fittings are built from a few shared parts, placed as copies. Each of some 10,000 copies was an object the processor looked after every frame: its place, whether it is in view, its lights, and its place sent to the graphics chip again.
- The copies that never move are now thin instances: one list of places per part, per floor and per area, sent to the graphics chip once when the airport opens. The airport goes from about 14,700 objects to about 4,800. In view from the kerb, the processor goes through about 2,300 objects a frame instead of about 8,900.
- The same number of chairs and everything else, in the same places, looking the same.
- Walking: each piece of furniture has an invisible box to bump into, so you stop at a row of seats and slide along it as before. Big pieces such as bar counters and security lanes have a box for each part, so you can still walk round and through them.
- The jetways, doors, lifts and everything else that moves or opens work as before.
