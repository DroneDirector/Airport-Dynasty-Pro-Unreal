# Airport Dynasty Pro (Unreal) 0.17.7

- **A new career starts in a new way.** New game on the title screen no longer drops you into the game's forms. It opens a dark neon room: she stands at the left, he at the right, two tall neon tubes between them and a fog over the floor lit by the lights. Point (mouse, arrow keys or the pad's stick) and one of them steps into the light; click, Enter or A chooses. The other turns and walks out of the picture, the tubes switch off and sink back into the fog, and the first panel slides in beside you — in the title screen's own graphics.
- **The steps.** Your looks, Your pet and Your partner show their panels already (they do nothing yet; the character creator, the pets and the partner come with later versions), then **Difficulty**, chosen here as the last step, then **Start game**. Every panel has Back; Back from the first one plays the scene in reverse (the tubes come forward, the other one walks back in to their place). Esc or B at the choice returns to the title screen. The title music plays on through all of it, with a mute button on every step, and fades out at Start game.
- **Your airline.** After Start game, the game's form asks only what it must: the kind of airline, its name, code and callsign, and your pilot's name. The home base is Mýrdalssandur (BIAP), our own airport in Iceland, always.
- **The loading screen.** From there a neon loading screen: a long animated bar, under it the file being loaded with the percentage at its end, and tips at the bottom. It stays until the game is in and showing, then the Overview.
- **No demo content.** There are no demo airlines any more, anywhere: a new career begins from nothing — no aircraft, no staff, no routes. You buy your aircraft and hire your crew. Continue with no career on this PC says so and New game sits first on the title screen.
- **Money by difficulty.** The starting money is measured against one used A320 (ten years old): on Hard that one aircraft, used, the crew it needs and a little spare; a touch more on Normal; plenty on Easy and Cruising along; less on Extreme. Regional and bush outfits are scaled to their aircraft.
- **Crew is always bought.** The first crew is no longer free, on any difficulty.
- **One aircraft to start.** Until your first aircraft has the crew it needs to fly, a second one — new, used or leased — is stopped with a message saying so. Hire the crew, then buy another whenever you can afford it.
- **Autosave** is on (every ten minutes) for every new career.
- **The office wardrobe.** A walnut wardrobe stands in every office. E at it opens the doors: the menu in the title screen's graphics, the categories on the left and the window of what you can wear beside it, and you stand to the right in the room, sharp, with the room gone soft behind you — the whole body, your face for hats and sunglasses, your feet for shoes. Only your character as built is in it so far; every outfit category finished later goes in here so you can try it on.
- **You, walking.** Your own character is now your walking body in the airport (your MetaHuman, with the walk and idle carried over to it).
- **Fire trucks.** The whole getting-in and getting-out pass: you walk over to the driver's door, reach the handle, the door swings open, you climb holding the handles and settle in; the engine has its own sound.
- **Grass distance** slider in Settings.
- **No-smoking stickers** on the entrance doors.
- **The airfield.** The ground under the runways, taxiways and aprons is graded smooth and seamless; the pavement drapes on it; the aircraft sit on the slope; windsock feet follow the grade; the concrete and asphalt edges get a soft band where they meet the ground (still being tuned).
- **People.** The builder puts one hairstyle on each person (no doubles), beards only where meant, heavier builds show as heavier, and the extras list has very overweight people and teenagers (some still to build).
